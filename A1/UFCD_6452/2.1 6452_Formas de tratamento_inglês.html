Formas de tratamento

How to address someone

UFCD 6452 — Eu e a minha rotina diária · Formadora: Sofia Coelho

1. Tu 2. Você 3. 3.ª pessoa 4. Vocês 5. Vós

1. Tu

informal

verb — 2nd person singular

Used with friends, family, children, young people, colleagues, people you are close to in general.

Exemplo Tu és português? /És português?* Are you Portuguese?
Tu falas inglês? /Falas inglês? Do you speak English?

*It is very common in Portuguese to drop the pronoun in a sentence.

2. Você

semiformal

verb — 3rd person singular

You wouldn't use it with a boss, someone in a position of power. Some people find it disrespectful and rude. Its usage is changing.

Exemplo Você gosta de brócolos? Do you like broccoli?

For now: avoid it. Use option 3 (3.ª pessoa).

3. 3.ª pessoa

formal

verb — 3rd person singular

When speaking to a neighbour / elder / boss / stranger, anyone you want to show respect to.

Options: (1) simply omit the pronoun «você»; (2) call the person by their name; (3) say «o senhor» or «a senhora»; (4) call the person by their title.

1 The most common way

Simply omit the pronoun «você» (null subject)

É de Lisboa? Are you from Lisbon?
Trabalha aqui? Do you work here?
2 Less formal (polite but friendly)

O / A + name

A Ana é portuguesa? Are you Portuguese? (addressing a person named Ana)
3 More formal

O senhor / A senhora (often used to address people middle-aged and over)

O senhor é o diretor dos Recursos Humanos? Are you the Director of Human Resources? (addressing an HR director)
A senhora mora aqui? Do you live here? (addressing a neighbour)
O senhor é o gerente? Are you the manager? (addressing the manager of a restaurant)
A senhora tem o passaporte? Do you have your passport? (a clerk at the Junta de Freguesia, addressing you)
4 Even more formal
  • O senhor diretor / A senhora diretora
  • O senhor presidente / A senhora presidente
  • O senhor doutor (Sr. Dr.) / A senhora doutora (Sr.ª Dr.ª)
  • O senhor engenheiro / A senhora engenheira
  • O senhor professor / A senhora professora
O senhor diretor é de Lisboa? Are you from Lisbon? (addressing a director)

Títulos

«Doutor» / «doutora» Até logo, Dr. João Silva.

Usually abbreviated to «Dr.» / «Dr.ª». It typically refers to anyone who is assumed to have any kind of post-secondary education.

«Professor Doutor» / «Professora Doutora»

To address a professor (PhD) in an academic situation.

«Dona» + first name or first name and surname D. Ana D. Ana Silva

Used before a woman's first name to address her, or refer to her, respectfully, especially when she's older.

«Senhor» + first name Sr. António

Used before a man's first name to address him, or refer to him, respectfully (the male equivalent of «Dona»). With first name and surname [«Sr. António Silva»] it is more formal.

«Senhora» + first name and surname Sra. Ana Silva

More formal than «Dona».

«Senhora Dona» + first name and surname Sra. D. Ana Silva

More formal than «Senhora»; used in formal invitations or official documents.

You already know two examples from «Cumprimentar e despedir-se»: Sr. António and D. Helena.

4. Vocês

speaking to more than one person

verb — 3rd person plural

Exemplo Vocês moram aqui? Do you live here?
Vocês trabalham aqui? Do you work here?
Vocês são de onde? Where are you from? (a teacher, addressing the class)

If you'd like to be formal, you may use:

os senhores as senhoras

5. Vós

speaking to more than one person

verb — 2nd person plural

It has almost fallen into disuse, except in the North of Portugal.

Exemplo Vós morais aqui? Do you live here?

Which one do I use?

friend, colleague of the same age, child→tu
older person, stranger, boss, client→3.ª pessoa (o senhor / a senhora, or no pronoun)
not sure?→3.ª pessoa, no pronoun
